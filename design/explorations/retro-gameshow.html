The concept

Every daily puzzle is a prime-time round, shot on a 1972 studio set.

Wordle's green / yellow / gray is recast as avocado, mustard and cocoa, so the board still reads at a glance. Around it: chase-light marquees, a split-flap scoreboard that clatters out your points, and a podium line-up for the week's contestants. Light mode is Studio lights; dark mode is After hours, with the bulbs left burning.

The system backstage

Every color is a token with a light (Studio lights) and dark (After hours) value. The chocolate "set" surface is dark in both themes. It is the stage the game happens on.

Each tile swings down on its hinge like a split-flap leaf (560ms, 180ms stagger) and lands with a small overshoot. Then the score clatters 00 to 80 and the marquee runs a fast chase. With reduced motion, tiles crossfade, the score jumps straight to 80 and the bulbs hold steady.

Producer's notes

Where the show earns its applause, and which dials to turn.

Signature moments

Cue sheet
  1. Result reveal. "And your score is…" The split-flap digits clatter from 00 to 80 in about a second while the sunburst behind them turns. A perfect solve gets a full-screen bulb chase and a starburst sticker.
  2. Share. The share card prints the lamps (▼ lower, ▲ higher, ★ got it) on a cream ticket stub. The group-chat text falls back to plain emoji so it still pastes like Wordle.
  3. Daily rollover. At midnight every countdown flap rolls to 0:00:00, the marquee goes to full chase, and the three doors close and reopen on new puzzles.
  4. Rank change. Podium nameplates swap places with a flap-board shuffle, and whoever climbed wears a mustard "+1" starburst for the rest of the day.
  5. Spoiler wall. Friends' results wait behind a velvet curtain. Finishing raises it (600ms ease-out), and the rows come on one at a time like contestants being introduced.
  6. Streaks. A price-tag starburst that gains rays at 7, 30 and 100 days. A broken streak doesn't go to zero quietly: the badge drops off the set.
