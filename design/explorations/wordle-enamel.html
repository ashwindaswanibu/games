Notes

Both themes get the same care. Porcelain is the default because tiles are an object you hold, and porcelain is where they read as an object.

Signature moments

  • Result reveal, “the firing”. Tiles flip on the X axis, 190 ms down and 190 ms up, 240 ms apart. The glaze is swapped while the tile is edge-on, so colour arrives as if it came out of the kiln. A solve adds one specular glint across the row and an 8 px weighted hop. No confetti: weight instead of sparkle.
  • The score plaque. Points are presented as a cobalt enamel badge with a brass inner rim. It is the one place the display serif sits on a tile, so it reads like a medal and is the natural screenshot.
  • Share. The share grid is made of the marks themselves: cobalt chevrons for lower and higher, a bottle-green check for the hit. The copied text falls back to emoji (⬇️⬆️🟩) so it still reads in any group chat.
  • Spoiler wall. Friends’ results sit under crazed, unfired glaze: milky, crackled, with a lock hallmark. Finishing “fires” the wall. The crackle clears and their rows flip in one by one, slower than your own reveal, so the payoff gets its own beat.
  • Daily rollover and rank change. At midnight the countdown’s digit tiles flip like a split-flap and finished cards go back to raw bisque for the new day. On the board, a rank tile that moves flips to its new number while the whole row slides as one slab.

Tweak knobs Four are live below

  • Glaze gloss. Matte stoneware at 0, wet lacquer at 1. Drives the highlight, reflection band and edge shading together.
  • Corner radius. 0 px reads as an enamel street sign, 12 px as a pebble of ceramic. 4 px keeps Wordle’s squareness.
  • Brass rim (cloisonné). A hairline of metal around every glazed tile. Off by default and kept for the plaque. Turned up, the whole app becomes jewellery.
  • Hallmarks. Opacity of the dot, ring and bar marks. Could stay subtle for everyone, with a high-contrast setting that makes them bold.
  • Distance from Wordle. How far the triad drifts from the original green, yellow and grey. Right now it is one step off: it reads as Wordle at thumbnail size without looking like a copy.
