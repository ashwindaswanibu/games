- [x] Search: "don" ranks *Don't Look Up* above *Don* (2006) because the search key splits "Don't" into "don t". Fix by treating an apostrophe as part of a word in `catalog_search_key` (SQL and TS together, with a migration that recomputes the keys). Built 2026-10-07 on `catalog-quality`, live 2026-10-07: every apostrophe-like character is dropped ("dont look up"), keys recomputed by `20261014000000_catalog_search_apostrophes`. No trade-off: names keep a second key, apostrophes as spaces (`split_key`, `20261014000200_catalog_search_split_key`), for their later words, so a surname after O' or D' is still a word of its own ("hara" → Catherine O'Hara, "connell" → Jack O'Connell, "souza" → Genelia D'Souza), while a start is judged by the joined key only (so "don" doesn't give Don't Look Up the whole-word bonus back). An 82-query evaluation (79 with an expected result) went from 74 to 79 as expected ("don" → Don first, "oneal" and "hara" find the O'Neals and O'Haras; the adult titles hidden; 78 with the joined key alone). Of the 40 people with an O'/D' surname and the most Wikipedia editions, the bare surname finds 30 in the top 8, the same 30 as with the old key (12 with the joined key alone). TS and SQL agree on both keys for all 270,871 stored titles and people but 4 (unrelated characters: Ɛ, ª, º and a combining half mark), and on the match class of 762,716 query–name pairs.
